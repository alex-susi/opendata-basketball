3. Audit of the weighting curve

Exact weighting and score formula
w(t) = 0 before −1.8; linear rise to 1 at −0.9;
1 through +0.24; linear fall to 0 at +0.5; 0 afterward.

Score = mean over screens i of [Σₜ w(t) × (−log pᵢ,ₜ(yᵢ)) / Σₜ w(t)].

What fixing the curve achieved

  • Anchors were fixed before tuning, without coverage labels or model predictions.
  • This guards against choosing a curve because it favors a particular model.
  • It does not establish the value of the prediction to a coach or player.

Closest point is not physical contact

  • Search-edge hits: 160/1,463 minima (10.9%) occur at the upper boundary, +0.48 seconds.
  • Next check: widen the search; the true closest moment may fall outside the current range.
  • Minimum H–S center distance: median 4.82 ft; 30.1% exceed 6 ft.
  • Interpretation: these values do not disprove a screen. They show why this variable should not be called physical contact.

The cutoff lacks a measured deadline

  • Handler’s touch ends: median +2.08 seconds; 97.8% end after +0.5 and 85.9% after +1.
  • But: pass or shot time is not necessarily the last useful time to read the defense.
  • Conclusion: the data do not establish a universal +0.5 deadline—or a replacement deadline of +2.08.

What the weights actually reward

  • Positive main weight: 57 of 76 prediction frames.
  • Before the tag: 77.3% of total main weight; at/after it: 22.7%.
  • After +0.5: no credit or penalty.
  • Narrow curve: 41.2% of weight falls at/after the tag. It is not a strictly early-forecast check.

The log-loss math is sound

  • A fixed, nonnegative weighted sum of log scores remains proper for the conditional probabilities at the included times.
  • Zero weights leave those times unjudged.
  • The weak point: which basketball task the weights represent.
  • Outcome-dependent timing weights can change the target; do not describe them as the same naturally calibrated five-class task.

Two design limits remain

  • Test-informed anchors: all ten games contributed, including eventual test games, although no coverage labels were used.
  • Confounded historical comparison: main-curve stopping and the move from two seeds to four happened together.
  • Rescoring limitation: today’s sensitivity scores use today’s checkpoints. They cannot show which checkpoints flat or pre-only stopping would select.

4. Audit of the −2 to +1 window and event labels

The start is a chosen forecast horizon

  • Approach already started: 628/1,444 measurable cases (43.5%) begin before −2 seconds.
  • Meaning: −2 is often partway through setup, rather than before any screen evidence.
  • Judgment: −2 can still be a valid chosen lead time. “Before the screen” is not one uniform information state.

Earlier frames contain more estimated positions

  • At −2: four-player tracking is available on about 98.5% of picks in the broader EDA.
  • Camera-detected positions: about 90.0% at −2 and 84.4% at −3; the rest are estimated.
  • Needed: explicit quality and missingness handling alongside any earlier prediction window.

The end is short for switch recognition, but extending blindly creates other errors

  • At +2: the EDA shows about 99.1% pointwise four-player availability.
  • Tracking availability alone does not explain the +1 stop.
  • Pointwise availability does not guarantee a valid full history—or that the same screen remains the current action.

A wider window can include a new action

  • Another pick: 41 graded screens have one within +1 second; 71 within +2.
  • Touch ends or next pick occurs: 245/1,463 (16.7%) by +1; 761 (52.0%) by +2.
  • These are boundary flags: a switch may remain relevant after a pass; a re-screen can change the defense.
  • Define: which event the displayed probability still describes.

Overlapping windows need clear labels

  • Overlap: 20.4% of graded screens have another pick within three seconds in either direction.
  • Held-out games: prevent this overlap from becoming train/test leakage.
  • Interpretation risk: the same court frame can be scored against two different eventual event labels.

Later is not automatically better

  • Both model curves: reach minimum loss around +0.4 to +0.5, then worsen toward +1.
  • Possible causes: the guard’s original route becomes less clear, or a later action enters the window.
  • Audit by: true coverage, touch status, re-screen, tracking quality and game.
  • Keep the late region visible: the curve was fixed before tuning, so this pattern does not prove a cutoff chosen to hide weak results.

Full-window completeness can distort a forecast cohort

  • Current inclusion rule: key positions must exist at every frame from −2 to +1, or the entire screen is excluded.
  • Excluded: 33/1,496 five-class screens—30 for early missingness, three for late possession/tracking loss.
  • Current impact: small; no large measured bias established.
  • Extension risk: widening the window increases future-based selection unless eligibility changes.

Score each prediction using its available past

  • Keep the present 1,463-screen cohort to reproduce the old result.
  • Add a causally eligible cohort with time-specific counts and quality flags; report both.
  • Do not require complete future data through +2 to score an earlier prediction.

Other timing assumptions deserve the same audit

Earlier labels become “known” at one second

  • KNOWN_AFTER <- 25L admits prior final labels into the defense’s “so far” history; current-screen labels are correctly excluded.
  • Assumption: an oracle supplies the earlier label after one second.
  • Audit: delays of 0/1/2/3 seconds and episode-end availability, plus a context-free control—not a search for the best-scoring delay.

Repair possession boundaries first

  • Some supplied ends precede the last chance or shot.
  • Use canonical viewer/bundle repairs; do not treat raw ends as reliable action cutoffs.

Check sensitivity to tag timing

  • Tag error can move observations between forecasting and recognition.
  • Review representative clips and test ±0.08, ±0.16 and ±0.24 seconds.
  • Do not optimize the shift against model scores.

The 1.6-second history still includes execution at +1

  • Input at +1: −0.6 through +1, including the tag and much of execution.
  • #5 may preserve simultaneous interactions, but “already forgot the screen at +0.5” is unsupported.

Keep timing definitions and denominators separate

The main score misses late recognition gains

  • #2 or #5 could improve confirmation around +0.8 or +1.2 without that gain appearing in the main score.
  • Early forecasting still matters: probabilities should remain calibrated and uncertain when the defense is not yet identifiable.

Why the current proxies need refinement

One-sided matchup handover

  • Checks: the screener’s defender takes the handler.
  • Does not require: the handler’s old defender takes the screener.
  • Also positive: 105/1,252 non-switches by +3 (8.4%); temporary shows, help or matchup tagging may contribute.

Tracking swap uses future persistence

  • Rule: the screener’s defender becomes closer to the handler and stays closer through the end of +2.
  • Consequence: retrospective timing that depends on the chosen endpoint.
  • Also positive: about 17% of non-switches.
  • Before live use: change the definition; do not use this unchanged as a detector or feature.

A stricter two-sided diagnostic

  • Rule: both assignments reverse together for at least 0.2 seconds.
  • Finds: 123/211 switches; conditional median +1.20. Also finds 17/1,252 non-switches by +3.
  • Tradeoff: lower sensitivity; these proxies do not supply one clean “true switch time.”
  • Causal confirmation: wait until persistence completes; the retrospective onset is not the live alert time.

Missing handover does not prove a wrong label

  • No handover proxy by +3: 53 graded switches.
  • Of those: 39 also have a touch end or next pick by +3.
  • Next step: review those clips before treating missing evidence or earlier boundaries as label error.

7. Recommended evaluation policy

Primary task: forecast the eventual coverage

  • Given: a candidate tagged on-ball screen and its four supplied participants.
  • Predict: the eventual five-class coverage using data available at the current frame.
  • Scope: conditional on that candidate until a separate screen detector is validated.

Secondary task: recognize the coverage

  • Confirm the same event’s defense as it unfolds.
  • State whether the card describes the original screen, a later re-screen or current possession-level matchups.
  • Show the difference between expected coverage and coverage being confirmed.

Later recognition needs two cohorts

  • Active episode: stop at a predefined observable transition—new pick, dead ball or possession change. Flag touch end separately.
  • Fixed-event follow-up: confirm the original label retrospectively; a pass need not end relevance. This is not a current-screen live card.
  • Before comparing scores: freeze both definitions.

A true utility curve needs a decision

  • Name the user and action: handler rejecting a screen, coach issuing a call, or scout tagging film.
  • Measure: relevant action time and the costs of the stated decision rule.
  • Limit: ten observational games cannot identify the causal effect of following model advice.
  • Until then: use horizon-specific prediction scores without claiming measured utility.

9. Implementing #4, #2 and #5

#4: let the two defenders’ non-switch decisions depend on each other

What the current outputs assume

  • The two defenders already share information and the common switch outcome.
  • After no switch, under/over and show/drop come from separate outputs using the same representation.
  • Remaining restriction: those decisions factor independently, conditional on the input and no switch.
#4 probability formulas
Current: p(U, H | x, not S) = p(U | x, not S) × p(H | x, not S).

Proposed outputs:
s = p(switch | x)
u = p(under | x, not switch)
hₒ = p(show | x, over, not switch)
hᵤ = p(show | x, under, not switch)

p(over,show) = (1−s)(1−u)hₒ
p(over,drop) = (1−s)(1−u)(1−hₒ)
p(under,drop) = (1−s)u(1−hᵤ)
p(under,show) = (1−s)u hᵤ
p(switch) = s.

Coach’s interpretation

  • The big’s show probability can depend on whether the guard goes over or under.
  • The model no longer needs one show probability for both possible routes.
  • One additional score permits any five-class distribution; it adds no tracking information.

Implement #4

  • Outputs: expand the non-switch branch from two logits to three. Retain the separate switch branch and its player-group inputs.
  • Update together: joint probabilities, defender marginals, loss, saved schema, loading and exports.
  • Initialize: copy the old show weights into both show outputs. Predictions must match the old model before training.
  • Train: through joint likelihood. Never supply the true under/over label as an inference input.

Validate #4

  • Numerics: probabilities sum to one; marginals equal joint sums; all five labels have finite loss.
  • Initialization: cloned outputs reproduce old predictions; gradients reach both show branches.
  • Measure: joint and defender loss, plus sparse under+show performance with uncertainty.
  • Limit: this does not specifically solve delayed switch evidence.

Why explicit relationships might help

  • The current Transformer can already learn interactions from positions, relative coordinates and nonlinear attention.
  • Deterministic pair features add an explicit representation, not new information.
  • The hoped-for gain is basketball structure and more efficient learning from ten games.

Implement #2 with minimal change

  • Compute all four pair streams at each historical timestamp using data available then.
  • Attach derived channels to the appropriate defender tokens: both defenders get their relationships with both attackers.
  • Preserve ten-player layout; use fold-training normalization and present/missing masks.
  • Keep player-group vectors in the switch branch only, so personnel routing stays fixed.

Add complexity only after that test

  • If useful but hard to interpret, test four typed relationship tokens or a shared pair encoder as a separate later variant.
  • A large graph network is not required for the first test.
  • Show measured distances and motion alongside probabilities; a learned pair score is not a causal explanation.

Validate #2

  • Geometry: matches raw coordinates and consistent rotation/reflection conventions.
  • Missingness: mask unavailable positions; do not turn them into artificial closeness.
  • Causality: scrambling future frames leaves present predictions unchanged.
  • Measure: early switch loss/calibration, later recognition and temporary-show false alarms. Handler-side evidence alone does not establish a completed exchange.

#5: compare players at each historical moment before summarizing time

Current: time first
  1. Summarize each player’s nine snapshots.
  2. Compare the ten player summaries.
  3. Read four screen-player summaries and context; groups also enter switch.
Proposed: players first
  1. Compare ten players at each of nine snapshots.
  2. Summarize interaction-aware player histories.
  3. Use the same four roles, context and outputs.

Coach’s interpretation

  • Today: summarize individual movement, then compare the summaries.
  • With #5: compare the five-on-five picture at every moment, then follow its changes.
  • Potential benefit: distinguish a brief show from a sustained exchange as the guard takes the roller.

Implement #5

  • Keep: nine steps, hidden size 32, four heads, one temporal layer and two player layers.
  • First: player attention at each timestamp, with weights shared across timestamps.
  • Then: temporal attention over role-consistent, interaction-aware player streams.
  • Cost: parameters can stay close or equal; runtime may rise because spatial layers process nine frames instead of one.

How #2, #4 and #5 fit together

  • #2 — inputs: make four assignment relationships explicit at each historical moment.
  • #5 — attention: follow how those relationships change after comparing each court picture.
  • #4 — outputs: express coordinated guard/big decisions.
  • Compatibility: they change different parts of the computation; test separately before combining.

Validate #5

  • Check: player identity across history, masks, position/role embeddings and no access after prediction time.
  • Trailing history: global attention is safe when predicting only at its end.
  • Intermediate-step predictions: need a causal mask if emitted in the same pass.
  • Attention display: show time and label it as information mixing, not causal attribution.

Reconstruction rules

  • Cohort: join the 1,463 anchor IDs to pick events; keep all graded events in timing denominators.
  • One-sided handover: first H–SD matchup interval intersecting −1:+3 in the same supplied possession; left-clamp at −1.
  • Two-sided exchange: also overlap S–BD for at least five frames. Retrospective onset checks future duration.
  • Other counts: overlap requires another pick strictly within three seconds; touch-end counts use 1,450 matched touches.
  • Possession ends: raw timestamps do not support cutoff recommendations because the project records known boundary errors.
