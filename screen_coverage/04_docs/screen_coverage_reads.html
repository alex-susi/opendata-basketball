Screen Coverage Reads

Can tracking data tell a ball handler how an on-ball screen will be defended, and how early?

  • Running record of the project. For a first look at the live model alone, read screen_coverage_model.html, also the site's Screen Coverage Model tab
  • Question, status and results first, then checks and next steps
  • Method details and math in collapsible folds near the end, dated changes last
10 tracked games 1,496 screens with a coverage · 1,463 graded 5 coverages every result held out by game past-only inputs, tested live · time-aware transformer v2

The question

Which coverage the two screen defenders will play, read from movement before the screen

  • SkillCorner's picks table records a coverage for both screen defenders on every on-ball screen
    • Ball handler's defender · over, under or switch
    • Screener's defender (the big) · show, drop or switch
  • Together they make one of five coverages (table below)
  • Ice (14 screens) and blitz (4) too rare to learn yet, left out of the models
  • Goal · a probability for each coverage every 0.04 s, using only what has already happened

Past-only on the clock

What a prediction at −0.4 s may and may not use

  • Movement read from −2.0 s to −0.4 s for each player
  • Closest-approach memory looks back from −2.4 s
  • A coverage joins tonight's tendencies 1 s after its own pick frame
    • So a re-screen's first coverage (pick frame −1.2 s) counts only from −0.2 s
  • Nothing right of the red line enters. 05_test_past_only.R checks this by scrambling it

Movement to a coverage

Five stages inside the transformer, from player histories to the five probabilities

1 · in10 players × 9 moments16 measures for each player at now, 0.2 s ago … 1.6 s ago
2 · each playerLooks backat his own last 1.6 s, sliding, turning, holding his level
3 · togetherPlayers comparedthe four in the screen take in where the other six are
4 · readScreen four + 9 numberstime to act, tonight's tendencies, closeness, closest-approach memory
5 · outThree questionsswitch? if not, under or over? show or drop? → the five coverages

Results

The main curve

How much each frame counts toward a score, by time from the pick frame

  • Why weight frames at all · a plain average over −2 s to +1 s rewards the wrong moments
    • Early frames · the screen is not yet coming, nobody could read the coverage
    • Late frames · the coverage is already on the floor, reading it is hindsight
  • The curve keeps full weight from when the screen is obvious to when the handler comes off it, the window where a read changes his decision
    • −1.8 s · handler and screener start closing on each other
    • −0.9 s · within 15 ft and staying there, the screen is obvious
    • +0.24 s · closest approach, the handler comes off the screener's shoulder
    • +0.5 s · about a quarter-second of reaction time later
  • Each screen's weights sum to 1, so every screen counts once
  • Used three ways
    • Stopping training on the validation game
    • The headline score (main curve column in Results)
    • The recall and precision matrices

The top-coverage rule
favours common coverages

Findings

What the tests so far say about reading screen coverage

Movement history pays
  • Against the logistic baseline, also never told when the screen comes
  • 0.059 better before the screen (8 of 10 held-out games)
  • 0.121 better after it (all 10)
No leak was doing the work
  • Rebuilt so nothing after the prediction moment can reach it
  • Before the screen 1.278 (was 1.276), after 0.760 (was 0.761), flat-stopping v2 vs v1
  • Both changes well inside the noise
Tonight's habits still count
  • Only coverages readable so far, no other game's data
  • Improve the logistic baseline before the screen by 0.038 (95% interval 0.011 to 0.065)
  • Better in 8 of 10 games
The big tips it early
  • Screener's defender read right 58.5% of the time before the screen (habit alone 51.5%)
  • Ball defender's over-or-under choice barely beats "expect over" (67.5% vs 67.0%) until the pick frame
  • Same pattern as the Fall–Marcos example
Switches read as over
  • 21% of switches and 19% of under + show caught by the top coverage (main curve)
  • 71% of switches read as an over coverage, even a second after the screen most still are
  • The model does rank switches higher (AUC 0.76), too weakly to make switch the top coverage
  • Switching depends on game plan and personnel the model never sees (Each coverage)
Helpers barely tip the big
  • Tested · each off-ball defender's distance from the screener's straight path to the rim, and how fast he closes on it
  • Big's score at −0.4 s · −0.004 (interval −0.016 to +0.011, 6 of 10 games). At −0.8 s · +0.000
  • Two more random starts of the unchanged model move it by −0.007
  • Main reason · the helpers' link to the big's coverage is weak
    • Nearest helper within 6 ft of the lane · more switches (19% vs 10–14%), less drop (38–40% vs 46–52%)
    • The gap alone predicts the big's coverage no better than no inputs on held-out games
    • Added to the logistic baseline, which cannot see the helpers, it gains only −0.002
Geometry adds nothing
  • Screen location, angle, side of the floor, re-screens and the clock did not help the five coverages
  • The defenders' own positions already carry them
  • Archived test, before the v2 rebuild

Limits

What to keep in mind before trusting a read

  • Small sample. Ten games. About 1,320 training screens per held-out game for the baseline, about 1,170 for the live model once its validation game is set aside
  • Vendor labels and positions. The coverages come from SkillCorner, and a staff might coverage some differently. Some positions are the vendor's estimates
  • The screen is known. Every model is told which four players are in the screen, which is only certain afterwards. A live system would also need to detect the screen coming
  • When a coverage becomes readable. Tonight's tendencies assume a coverage is known 1 s after its pick frame. A judgment coverage, since the data has no "readable" time
  • Drift after the screen. Even with closest-approach memory, the read slips back after about +0.5 s
    • Log loss 0.73 at +0.4 s, 0.87 at +1 s (03_outputs/timing_compare/by_time.csv, "none")
    • Training now stops on moments up to +0.5 s only, so the late drift grew a little (was 0.72 and 0.84)
  • Percentages honest from about −0.5 s on. Checked in 15_calibration.R
    • Earlier, the leading coverage stated too high (8 points at −2 s, 5 at −1 s)
    • After the pick frame, about 5 points too low
    • A confident switch read (60%+) before the pick frame comes true only about a third of the time (21 screens from −0.5 s to the pick frame)
  • Attention rings explain little. Attention is spread almost evenly across the ten players
  • What 55% means. A lean toward the likely coverage. Too uncertain to bet on
  • Rare coverages mostly missed. Switch 21% and under + show 19% caught by the top coverage. The card's Combined coverage almost always names an over coverage (see Each coverage)

  • Film check · watch switches still read as over at +1 s. If the defenders never trade players on film, the label is the ceiling. If they do, the inputs miss something visible
  • Who guards whom after the screen · nearest defender to the handler and to the screener as direct inputs. A switch is that trade
  • Personnel · each player's season share of picks as handler or screener and three-point attempt rate, the two that moved switch and under above. Season aggregates include these ten games, so the owner decides whether offense-only usage counts as a leak. Never the coverage columns (_vs_switch and the like)
  • Defender types from archetypes/ (a big who can guard a guard switches more)
  • Card · show switch risk when its probability is well above its usual share, keeping the probabilities unchanged
  • Class weights in training · lifts rare-coverage recall but bends the probabilities, so the main-curve score would need a separate check
Model structure
  • Longer history (2–3 s) or an explicit "the screen has happened" detector, to stop the drift after the screen
  • A version on the four screen players only, to separate the architecture's gain from the other six players' gain
  • An ice detector as a separate output once more games are available
Training and tuning
  • Small search over width, layers, heads, dropout and learning rate, scored on the pooled held-out main-curve score
  • Picking per fold on one validation game is unreliable. Twice on 2026-11-15 a version won 7 of 10 validation games and showed no test gain
  • Four random starts now averaged
  • One temperature for all moments would barely help (1.1143 → 1.1137) and make after-screen reads more underconfident
  • A softening that depends on time from the pick frame fits the calibration pattern
  • Pretraining on all tracking frames (e.g. predicting where players move next) before learning the coverages
New inputs
  • Ball position and height
  • Personnel threat · the screener's pop rate and the handler's pull-up rate, built without leaking the season's own outcomes
  • Dropped after testing (2026-11-15) · two-step time-to-screen (the model already reads timing from movement) · speed toward the hoop (implied by distance to the hoop at nine moments) · help-defender lane, pop and drive measures
  • If lanes are revisited · a pass gap that ignores defenders beside the handler, and the screener's shooting threat
Going live and checking
  • A screen-detection step ("a screen is coming, from this teammate") so the card switches on without the picks table
  • Results by team, screen location and shot clock
  • Calibration curves added 2026-11-15 (03_outputs/calibration/)
  • A test on new games, since ten games may not carry over to other matchups

The model
  • At every frame from −2 s to +1 s, 26 numbers about the screen → a probability for each of the five coverages
  • Multinomial logistic regression, over + show as the reference coverage
P(y=k∣x)=exp(βk0+βk⊤x~)∑j=15exp(βj0+βj⊤x~),βover + show=0 x~f=xf−μfσf,x∈ℝ26

The 26 inputs

  • 11 four-player measures (distances, levels, how fast the levels change, the screener's distance from the hoop, time to act)
  • Logs of the defense's five readable coverage shares so far
  • Closeness c=1/(1+dhs/5)
  • 9 of the measures × closeness, so a position can count more as the handler reaches the screen without the model being told the time

Fitting

  • Means μf and spreads σf from the training games only
  • Maximises the weighted log-likelihood minus an L2 penalty (nnet decay = 1)
  • Every frame weighted 1/76, so each screen counts once however many frames it has
  • One fit per held-out game, on the other nine
Which inputs it relies on
  • Each held-out game predicted twice, as is and with one input group shuffled across the game's rows
  • Shuffling breaks that input's link to the coverage while keeping its values realistic
  • Rise in log loss = how much the model relies on that input
  • A measure and its "× closeness" twin shuffled together. The five coverage shares are one group
  • Five shuffles per group, averaged. Mean over the 10 games, 95% interval from resampling games (first column)

Live transformer mathEvery tensor, weight matrix and formula from the raw measures to the five probabilities, and how it is trained

Notation

  • B · number of prediction rows in a batch
  • p = 1…10 · a player (1 handler, 2 screener, 3 handler's defender, 4 screener's defender, 5-7 other attackers, 8-10 other defenders)
  • t = 1…9 · a history step (1 = now, 9 = 1.6 s ago)
  • d = 32 · width of every hidden vector
  • All shapes from R/seq_transformer.R and the saved networks

1 · Inputs and standardisation
  • Each prediction row is a tensor X∈ℝ10×9×16 (players × steps × measures) and a context vector c∈ℝ9
  • The 16 measures · x, y, position relative to the screener and to the handler, distance from the hoop, level, velocity, speed, speeding up, turning, level changing, time held at a level, present
  • First, y-type columns multiplied by −1 when needed so the screener is on the y ≥ 0 side at the prediction frame
  • Then each measure standardised with the training screens only
x~p,t,f=xp,t,f−μfσf,c~m=cm−μmσm
  • μf and σf taken over present player-frames of the eight training games
  • Mirrored columns keep μf=0 so their sign survives
  • "Present" keeps μ=0,σ=1
  • A missing player has raw zeros and present = 0
2 · Embedding · measures to 32-wide vectors
hp,t(0)=Wex~p,t+be+st+rρ(p) We∈ℝ32×16,st∈ℝ32(t=1…9),rρ∈ℝ32(6 roles)
  • A linear map turns each player's 16 measures at one step into a 32-wide vector
  • st · learned vector saying which step it is (now, 0.2 s ago, …)
  • r · learned vector saying which role the player has
  • ρ maps players 5-7 to "other attacker" and 8-10 to "other defender", so those six share embeddings and their order does not matter
3 · One attention layer (used three times)
  • Takes n rows H∈ℝn×32 (9 steps of one player, or 10 players), returns rows of the same shape
  • 4 heads of width dk=32/4=8
  • Normalises before each part ("pre-norm") and adds each part back onto its input
Z=LN(H),Qj=ZWjQ,Kj=ZWjK,Vj=ZWjV(j=1…4,32×8each, with biases) Aj=softmax(QjKj⊤8)∈ℝn×n,O=[A1V1∣…∣A4V4]WO H′=H+O,H″=H′+ReLU(LN(H′)W1+b1)W2+b2 LN(u)=γ⊙u−u‾su2+ε+β,W1∈ℝ32×64,W2∈ℝ64×32

In words

  • Each row asks a question (query), every row offers a label (key)
  • The softmax of their dot products gives weights that sum to 1 across the rows
  • The row receives that weighted average of the rows' values
  • Each of the 4 heads can look for something different. WO (32 × 32) mixes them back together
  • A small two-layer network then works on each row alone
  • During training, dropout (rate 0.2) zeroes a random fifth of the attention weights and of each part's output
4 · Attention along time, then across players
up=[Layertime(hp,1:9(0))]t=1∈ℝ32,U=[u1;…;u10]∈ℝ10×32 U′=Layerplayers(2)(Layerplayers(1)(U))∈ℝ10×32

Along time

  • One layer with its own weights, shared by all ten players
  • Each player's 9 steps attend to each other, a 9 × 9 weight matrix per head
  • Only his "now" row is kept. It now carries a summary of his last 1.6 s (sliding, turning, holding a level)

Across players

  • Two layers, each with its own weights
  • The ten "now" rows attend to each other, a 10 × 10 weight matrix per head
  • The Model Attention toggle shows the last layer's weights for rows 1-4, averaged over heads
  • No row is masked, so every player sees every player

g=[LN(u1′);LN(u2′);LN(u3′);LN(u4′);c~]∈ℝ137 ℓ=WbReLU(Wag+ba)+bb=(ℓsw,ℓun,ℓsh),Wa∈ℝ64×137,Wb∈ℝ3×64 psw=σ(ℓsw),pun=σ(ℓun),psh=σ(ℓsh),σ(z)=11+e−z

switchpsw
  • Sees only the four screen players' rows (which already took in the other six through attention) and the nine context numbers
  • Three scores = three yes/no questions · switch? if not, did the ball defender go under? did the big show?
  • The five probabilities always sum to 1
  • Each defender's own coverage follows by adding
    • Big shows =(1−psw)psh
    • Guard goes under =(1−psw)pun
  • The two defenders share psw, and under + show uses the same pun and psh as every other coverage, so the rare joint coverages borrow strength from the common ones
