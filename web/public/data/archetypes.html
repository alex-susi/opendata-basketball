Playstyle archetypes

Every player is described by how he plays, not by how well: where his shots come from, how he uses the ball and screens, where he stands, whom he guards. Players with similar habits are grouped into offensive and defensive archetypes, and each player gets a probability of belonging to each one.

How the archetypes are built

  1. Measure tendencies, not results

    130 features describe what a player does and where he is on the floor: 93 on offense and 37 on defense. Season features come from the shot, drive and pick aggregates (293 games); tracked features come from the events and tracking of the ten sample games. Makes, points, percentages, assists, turnovers, fouls drawn, blocks, steals and rebounds collected are all left out.

  2. Keep only what repeats

    Each feature's noise is estimated (from odd against even chances for tracked features) and every player's value is pulled toward the league average in proportion to how little he was observed. A feature enters the clustering only if its median reliability among well-sampled players is at least 0.7: 45 offensive and 20 defensive features pass.

  3. Reduce to principal components

    The surviving features are heavily correlated, so they are combined into principal components. A component is kept only if it carries more variance than the same component of shuffled data. That keeps 4 components on offense (72% of the variance) and 2 on defense (77%).

  4. Fit a mixture model on the well-sampled players

    Clusters are defined by the players seen enough to trust: 169 on offense (25+ tracked chances and 30+ season attempts) and 139 on defense (40+ tracked chances). Fourteen Gaussian mixture types were compared at 2 to 12 clusters on fit (BIC), on how well the clusters survive refitting on resampled players, on cluster size and, on offense, on how closely the clusters resemble BBall Index's offensive roles. The solution in use was chosen from that comparison. Offense: 10 clusters as round clusters of equal size (mclust model EII). Defense: 4 clusters as equal-sized clusters sharing one axis-aligned shape (mclust model EEI).

  5. Give every player a probability of every archetype

    All 310 players in the season aggregates get offensive probabilities and the 205 tracked players get defensive ones. Each player is compared with every cluster using his raw values and his own sampling noise, and features he has no data for are left out, so thinly observed players get flatter probabilities instead of confident labels.

Offensive archetypes

The numbers under "What sets it apart" are how far the archetype sits from the league average on that feature, in standard deviations.

Off Screen Shooter

20 players · 15 well-sampled · mean probability 0.76

A shooter who works off screens. A large share of his shots are jumpers off movement, his drives start from off-ball screens and most of his attempts are threes; he rarely scores in the paint. He does less off the dribble than a secondary ball handler.

Most typical: Matt Thomas, Mark Hughes, Erik Stevenson, Kyle Kuric, Alfonso Plummer

What sets it apart

  • +1.6 share of attempts that are jumpers off movement
  • +1.4 share of drives that start from an off-ball screen
  • +1.2 off-ball screens used as cutter per 100 chances
  • +0.9 share of attempts that are above-the-break threes
  • -0.9 share of attempts that are layups
  • -0.8 share of attempts in the restricted area

Movement Shooter

52 players · 26 well-sampled · mean probability 0.78

A floor spacer who shoots off the catch, often after relocating. He takes corner and above-the-break threes, including jumpers off movement, and when he puts the ball on the floor it is to attack a closeout. He seldom scores in the paint or handles in pick-and-roll. The largest group; it includes most of the shooting forwards that BBall Index would call stretch bigs.

Most typical: Aaron Doornekamp, Yves Pons, Mihajlo Andric, Mindaugas Susinskas, Toni Nakic

What sets it apart

  • +1.3 share of attempts that are corner threes
  • +1.3 share of drives that start from attacking a closeout
  • +0.9 share of attempts that are catch-and-shoot (rest are off the dribble)
  • +0.9 share of off-ball half-court time spent in: corner
  • -0.9 share of attempts in the paint outside the restricted area
  • -0.9 share of attempts that are layups

Stationary Shooter

21 players · 11 well-sampled · mean probability 0.72

A low-usage spacer who waits in the corner. He stands still off the ball more than anyone, shoots off the catch and attacks closeouts all the way to the rim. He takes few shots and touches the ball little.

Most typical: Bakary Dibba, Jaime Fernandez Manzanares, Sergi Martinez Costa, Thomas Scrubb, Henri Drell

What sets it apart

  • +1.2 share of drives that start from attacking a closeout
  • +1.0 share of attempts that are corner threes
  • +1.0 share of off-ball half-court time spent in: corner
  • +0.8 share of off-ball half-court time moving slower than 2 ft/s
  • -0.9 share of off-ball half-court time spent in: top
  • -0.9 share of drives that start from an on-ball screen

Athletic Finisher

36 players · 22 well-sampled · mean probability 0.76

A wing or forward who scores going to the basket. His drives end at the rim with layups and contested finishes, and he takes few pull-ups or off-the-dribble threes. This is the least sharply defined archetype: it sits near the middle of the two main components.

Most typical: Leonardo Simoes Meindl, Howard Sant-Roos, Nihad Djedovic, Agustin Ubal, Santiago Yusta

What sets it apart

  • +0.6 share of drives ending in a shot near the basket
  • +0.5 share of attempts that are layups
  • +0.4 share of off-ball half-court time spent in: corner
  • +0.4 share of off-ball half-court time moving slower than 2 ft/s
  • -0.5 share of off-ball half-court time spent in: top
  • -0.5 share of drives ending in a pull-up

Primary Ball Handler

45 players · 26 well-sampled · mean probability 0.88

Runs the offense. He brings the ball up, handles in pick-and-roll far more than anyone else, holds the ball longest and takes most of his shots off the dribble, pull-up threes included. He rarely spots up, sets screens or goes to the offensive glass.

Most typical: Shannon Evans, Kendrick Perry, Marco Spissu, Dominik Mavra, Ricky Rubio

What sets it apart

  • +2.0 direct picks as ball handler per 100 possessions on court
  • +1.9 share of chances (second chances excluded) where he has the ball when it first reaches the frontcourt
  • +1.8 dribbles per touch
  • +1.5 share of his team's on-court offensive clock time with the ball in his hands
  • -1.4 share of touches with no dribble
  • -1.4 share of attempts that are catch-and-shoot (rest are off the dribble)

Post Scorer

29 players · 15 well-sampled · mean probability 0.79

A big who scores with his back to the basket and around the rim. He has the highest share of hooks and post fadeaways, takes many layups and paint shots, and sets on-ball and off-ball screens. He does not shoot threes or play on the wing.

Most typical: Ethan Happ, Willy Hernangomez, Ante Tomic, Luka Bozic, Dragan Apic

What sets it apart

  • +1.6 direct picks as screener per 100 possessions on court
  • +1.6 share of off-ball half-court time spent in: mid
  • +1.6 share of attempts that are hooks or post fadeaways
  • +1.5 share of attempts in the restricted area
  • -1.6 share of attempts that are above-the-break threes
  • -1.4 share of off-ball half-court time spent in: wing

Slasher

29 players · 13 well-sampled · mean probability 0.85

An on-ball guard whose first aim is the rim. He drives more than any other archetype, mostly out of pick-and-roll, and starts a fair share of possessions, but handles less and takes fewer pull-up jumpers than a primary ball handler.

Most typical: Matteo Spagnolo, Theo Maledon, Joaquin Nazar Rodriguez Olivera, Omari Moore, Jon Axel Gudmundsson

What sets it apart

  • +1.4 drives per 100 possessions on court
  • +1.1 share of chances (second chances excluded) where he has the ball when it first reaches the frontcourt
  • +1.0 direct picks as ball handler per 100 possessions on court
  • +1.0 mean distance from the hoop off the ball in the half court, ft
  • -1.1 share of attempts that are catch-and-shoot (rest are off the dribble)
  • -1.0 share of touches with no dribble

Secondary Ball Handler

30 players · 18 well-sampled · mean probability 0.79

A scoring guard who creates his own shot without running the offense. He takes a high volume of shots, many of them pull-ups and off-the-dribble threes and many of them contested, and often gets the ball coming off an off-ball screen. He starts fewer possessions than a primary ball handler and seldom gets to the rim.

Most typical: Kevin Punter, Chris Duarte, Justin Wright-Foreman, Darrun Hilliard, Jhivvan Jameel Jackson Melendez

What sets it apart

  • +1.4 off-ball screens used as cutter per 100 chances
  • +1.2 share of attempts that are self-created jumpers (pull-up, stepback, shake-and-raise, leaner)
  • +1.2 share of attempts that are off-the-dribble threes
  • +1.1 field goal attempts per 100 possessions on court
  • -0.8 share of attempts that are catch-and-shoot (rest are off the dribble)
  • -0.8 share of teammates' shots where he is on the glass when the ball reaches the rim

Roll & Cut Big

36 players · 16 well-sampled · mean probability 0.73

A big who finishes above the rim. Dunks, lobs and tips make up an unusual share of his shots, he sets the most on-ball screens and spends his off-ball time in the paint. The name describes the role: counts of rolls and cuts were too noisy to be clustering inputs.

Most typical: Kaodirichi Akobundu-Ehiogu, Martinas Geben, Danko Brankovic, Mike Tobey, Bassala Bagayoko

What sets it apart

  • +1.7 share of attempts that are dunks or lobs
  • +1.7 direct picks as screener per 100 possessions on court
  • +1.6 share of off-ball half-court time spent in: paint
  • +1.3 share of attempts in the restricted area
  • -1.5 share of off-ball half-court time spent in: wing
  • -1.2 feet from the hoop where his frontcourt touches start

Versatile Big

12 players · 7 well-sampled · mean probability 0.78

A forward or big who does a bit of everything inside the arc. He posts up, shoots from mid-range, sets screens, goes to the offensive glass and usually finishes without a dribble. The smallest group, and unlike BBall Index's versatile big it is not defined by three-point shooting.

Most typical: Pierre Pelos, Augustine Rubit, Emir Sulejmanovic, Mamadi Diakite, Beka Burjanadze

What sets it apart

  • +0.9 share of attempts that are hooks or post fadeaways
  • +0.8 share of off-ball half-court time spent in: mid
  • +0.8 share of touches with no dribble
  • +0.6 share of attempts from mid-range
  • -1.0 share of drives that start from an on-ball screen
  • -0.8 share of attempts that are off-the-dribble threes
  • Stability: 0.62. This is the agreement between these clusters and the ones found when the model is refitted on resampled players (1 = identical; good to about ±0.1).
  • Membership certainty: a player's most likely archetype has probability 0.79 on average; 24 of 310 players are under 0.5.
  • Fit: BIC -2847, against -2734 for the best-fitting alternative (VEV with 3 clusters), which was judged too coarse to describe playstyles.

Offensive components

Each component is a weighted mix of the features. The lists show the features with the largest weights on each end.

Component 1

42% of variance

The main split in the league: who has the ball and who plays off it.

− On-ball creator

Handles in pick-and-roll, brings the ball up, dribbles a lot per touch and shoots off the dribble. Typical of point guards.

  • -0.24 Direct picks as ball handler per 100 possessions on court
  • -0.23 Share of attempts that are self-created jumpers (pull-up, stepback, shake-and-raise, leaner)
  • -0.22 Share of attempts that are off-the-dribble threes
  • -0.22 Dribbles per touch

+ Off-ball screener and finisher

Catches and shoots or finishes without a dribble, sets screens and goes to the offensive glass. Typical of bigs.

  • +0.21 Share of touches with no dribble
  • +0.20 Share of attempts that are catch-and-shoot (rest are off the dribble)
  • +0.18 Share of off-ball half-court time spent in: mid
  • +0.18 Share of teammates' shots where he is on the glass when the ball reaches the rim

Component 2

19% of variance

Among players off the ball, where they operate: outside or inside.

− Interior scorer

Scores in the paint with layups, floaters and post moves, and spends his off-ball time at the top of the floor as a screener.

  • -0.26 Share of off-ball half-court time spent in: top
  • -0.26 Share of attempts in the paint outside the restricted area
  • -0.22 Share of attempts that are layups
  • -0.17 Share of attempts in the restricted area

+ Perimeter spacer

Lives beyond the arc, especially in the corners, shoots off the catch or off movement and attacks closeouts.

  • +0.29 Share of attempts that are corner threes
  • +0.28 Share of off-ball half-court time spent in: corner
  • +0.25 Share of attempts that are jumpers off movement
  • +0.24 Share of drives that start from attacking a closeout

Component 3

6% of variance

How a player gets his points: jump shots in volume, or occasional attacks on the rim.

− High-volume jump-shot scorer

Takes many shots, with pull-ups, mid-range jumpers and shots coming off off-ball screens.

  • -0.35 Share of drives ending in a pull-up
  • -0.33 Share of attempts from mid-range
  • -0.30 Field goal attempts per 100 possessions on court
  • -0.29 Off-ball screens used as cutter per 100 chances

+ Low-usage rim attacker

Shoots little, and mostly layups; his drives end at the rim and he stands still off the ball.

  • +0.25 Share of attempts that are layups
  • +0.18 Share of off-ball half-court time moving slower than 2 ft/s
  • +0.17 Share of off-ball half-court time spent in: corner
  • +0.16 Share of drives ending in a shot near the basket

Component 4

5% of variance

Players who hunt their own shot on the wing against those who organise from the top.

− Organiser

Stays at the top of the floor, starts possessions, runs pick-and-roll in the middle and shoots off the catch.

  • -0.23 Share of off-ball half-court time spent in: top
  • -0.22 Share of attempts that are catch-and-shoot (rest are off the dribble)
  • -0.16 Share of direct picks as handler set in the middle of the floor
  • -0.15 Share of chances (second chances excluded) where he has the ball when it first reaches the frontcourt

+ Isolation and off-screen wing scorer

Drives from isolation, takes contested shots, gets to the rim and shoots a lot.

  • +0.35 Share of non-blocked attempts taken under an average or heavy contest
  • +0.35 Share of drives that start from an isolation
  • +0.31 Share of drives ending in a shot near the basket
  • +0.30 Field goal attempts per 100 possessions on court

Defensive archetypes

The numbers under "What sets it apart" are how far the archetype sits from the league average on that feature, in standard deviations.

Point of Attack

35 players · 27 well-sampled · mean probability 0.88

Guards the ball. He is assigned to the opposing ball handlers, spends the largest share of his time on the ball, defends the handler in pick-and-roll and picks the ball up furthest from the basket.

Most typical: Facundo Campazzo, Brancou Badio, Trae Bell-Haynes, Theo Maledon, David Dejulius

What sets it apart

  • +1.6 season direct picks as handler per 100 possessions of the men he is assigned
  • +1.5 on-ball screens defended as the handler's defender per 100 chances
  • +1.5 share of assigned half-court time in which his man has the ball
  • +1.5 mean distance from the hoop on defense in the half court, ft
  • -1.2 share of half-court defensive time as the defender nearest the hoop
  • -0.9 share of half-court defensive time in the paint

Helper

75 players · 54 well-sampled · mean probability 0.94

A wing or forward who defends away from the ball. He is assigned mostly to three-point shooters, plays furthest from his own man and spends the most time in help position, and is often the defender nearest the rim when the big is drawn away.

Most typical: Luka Bozic, Devin Robinson, Nico Brussino, Sergi Martinez Costa, Aleksandar Aranitovic

What sets it apart

  • +0.6 feet from his man when his man is off the ball
  • +0.6 share of off-ball time spent 8+ ft from his man
  • +0.5 share of half-court defensive time as the defender nearest the hoop
  • +0.5 season three-point share of the men he is assigned, weighted by time
  • -0.7 share of half-court defensive time 18+ ft from the hoop
  • -0.7 mean speed on defense in the half court, ft/s

Chaser

46 players · 31 well-sampled · mean probability 0.86

A perimeter defender whose man is usually off the ball. He follows cutters and shooters through off-ball screens and handoffs, stays out on the perimeter with his man and takes some turns guarding the ball.

Most typical: Kassius Robertson, Arturs Kurucs, Yves Pons, Ziga Samar, Agustin Ubal

What sets it apart

  • +0.7 on-ball screens defended as the handler's defender per 100 chances
  • +0.6 mean distance from the hoop of the man he is assigned, ft
  • +0.6 feet from the hoop where he first gets within 8 ft of the ball handler he is guarding
  • +0.6 off-ball screens and handoffs defended as the cutter's or receiver's defender per 100 chances
  • -0.7 season direct picks as screener per 100 possessions of the men he is assigned
  • -0.6 share of half-court defensive time in the paint

Anchor Big

49 players · 27 well-sampled · mean probability 0.97

The interior defender. He guards the screeners, defends the screener's side of pick-and-roll and off-ball screens, stays in the paint, helps on drives and is on the defensive glass. He stays close to his own man, who is near the basket.

Most typical: Tryggvi Hlinason, Martinas Geben, Aleksander Balcerowski, Mike Tobey, Ethan Happ

What sets it apart

  • +1.8 season direct picks as screener per 100 possessions of the men he is assigned
  • +1.5 share of half-court defensive time in the paint
  • +1.5 on-ball screens defended as the screener's defender per 100 chances
  • +1.1 share of other men's drives where he closes 4+ ft to end within 6 ft of the driver
  • -1.6 season three-point share of the men he is assigned, weighted by time
  • -1.3 mean distance from the hoop of the man he is assigned, ft
  • Stability: 0.90. This is the agreement between these clusters and the ones found when the model is refitted on resampled players (1 = identical; good to about ±0.1).
  • Membership certainty: a player's most likely archetype has probability 0.92 on average; 1 of 205 players are under 0.5.
  • Fit: BIC -1135, against -1124 for the best-fitting alternative (VVV with 3 clusters), which was judged too coarse to describe playstyles.

Limits

  • The archetype names and the wording on this page are a reading of the numbers. The model only finds the groups.
  • Players lie along a continuum more than in separate clumps, so the borders between neighbouring archetypes are soft. The probabilities say how close a player is to a border.
  • Defensive roles rest on one or two tracked games per player, and so reflect that opponent and game plan. There is no season-long defensive data.
  • Probabilities are optimistic about season-long role, because noise in the tracked features is measured within games.
  • Several intended features were too noisy to use, including roll or pop after a screen, cuts, post-ups, isolations and the coverage a big plays against the pick-and-roll. The three kinds of big are therefore told apart by their shots and where they stand, not by what they do after a screen.
  • Ten offensive archetypes is more than the data settles firmly: the offensive stability score above is lower than it would be for fewer, broader groups, and BBall Index's shot creator and stretch big do not appear as groups of their own.

Data: SkillCorner open data, Liga ACB 2025-2026. Inspired by BBall Index's offensive archetypes and defensive roles.
