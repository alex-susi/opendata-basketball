Court Control

For every 2 ft spot on the floor, which team would get to the ball first if it were passed there right now.

  • A basketball version of soccer's pitch control (Spearman 2016, 2017, Fernandez and Bornn 2018)
  • Built from the ten players' positions and velocities, the ball's position and each player's own speed limits
  • Fitted on 3,728 live passes and checked one game at a time
10 tracked games 2 ft cells · 1,150 per court every frame of live play held-out pass test in the play viewer

The question

Who owns each part of the floor at this moment

  • A ball passed to a spot gets there after a flight time. The first player to reach it takes it
  • Do this for every spot and the floor splits into ground the offense would win and ground the defense would win
  • Pale cells are toss-ups, deep colours are safe for one team
  • Use · open passing lanes, how well the offense spaces, how much floor a rotation gives up, and the offense's share of its half
  • Descriptive only. It says who could reach a pass, nothing about where the ball should go

One spot, five steps

How the model scores a single cell. Every cell on the court is scored the same way, every frame

  1. Ball time

    • tball = 0.016 s + 0.0201 s per ft × distance from the ball to the cell
    • About 50 ft per s, measured from the ten games' passes

  2. Each player's arrival time

    • He keeps his current velocity for a reaction time of 0.05 s
    • Then heads straight for the cell. His speed toward it changes no faster than his own acceleration, up to his own sprint speed
    • Moving away, he first has to stop, at his own braking rate, which carries him further away
    • Sideways velocity is ignored

  3. Uncertain arrival

    • By time t, he has arrived with probability 1 / (1 + exp(−(t − arrival) · π / (√3 · 0.45 s)))
    • A logistic with a spread of 0.45 s, so a player a few tenths behind still has a chance

  4. The race for the ball

    • From the moment the ball arrives, while nobody has it, every player who has arrived takes it at 50 per s
    • Stepped every 0.04 s until someone holds it with 99% certainty (at most 6 s)
    • So the first to arrive nearly always wins, and a near tie splits

  5. The cell's control

    • The offense's five players' summed chances of taking it, out of all ten
    • Coloured in the offense's colour above 0.5, the defense's below, paler near 0.5

  • A defender closing at speed beats a standing attacker who is nearer, because the attacker still has to get going
  • Moving away costs more than standing still. He has to stop before he can come back
  • A long pass gives both players time, so the spot goes to whoever arrives first by a clear margin

Each player's limits

Every player runs at his own measured speed, acceleration and braking

LimitHow it is measured
Sprint speedHis 99th percentile of live-play speed (velocity over 6 frames, capped at 30 ft per s). Scaled so the average player runs at 18.1 ft per s, the speed the model was first fitted with. Range 16.4 to 20.0 ft per s
AccelerationHis 99th percentile of speed gained between velocities 0.24 s apart. League 12.4 ft per s²
BrakingThe same for speed lost. League 11.9 ft per s²
Small samplesEach limit is pulled toward his offensive archetype's average, with the weight of 10 live minutes
  • Centres come out slowest, off-ball wings fastest
  • It is observed speed, so it mixes ability with role
  • Figures from web/scripts/sample_stats.R ("Motion limits"), stored in sample.json

Fitting and checks

The reaction time, spread and claim rate are chosen by how well control at the catch spot separates completed passes from interceptions

  • 3,728 live passes in the ten games, 66 intercepted (1.8%)
    • No inbounds, caught within 2 s, five tracked players a side and the ball in view
    • All ten players' positions and velocities at release, and the spot where the pass was taken
  • Control at that spot, recalibrated by a two-parameter logistic, scored by log loss
  • The logistic absorbs passer choice. Passers pick safe passes, so raw control overstates the risk
  • Grid search over reaction time, spread and claim rate, one game held out at a time

In the play viewer

Computed live in the browser for the frame on screen

  • Display › Court control shades every cell in the two teams' colours
  • The readout gives the offense's share of the whole court or of the half it attacks
  • Needs five players a side and the ball in view. Otherwise the layer is left off for that frame

Limits

What the model leaves out

  • Straight lines. Players run straight at each spot. No cutting around a body, no screens in the way
  • No hands or bodies. A deflection, a lob over a defender or a bounce pass is the same as a chest pass
  • Few interceptions. 66 in ten games, so the fit is coarse
  • Limits use all ten games. Each player's motion limits include the held-out game's tracking (movement only, never pass outcomes)
  • Tracking error. About 1 in 5 positions are vendor estimates for players off camera
  • Not where to pass. Owning a spot says nothing about whether a pass there would lead to a good shot
